### 1b. Outlook email stuck in Outbox
**Best for groups like:** Microsoft Office tips and help, small business owners, office admins.

Email stuck in the Outlook Outbox? Microsoft names a large attachment as the most likely cause, and clicking Send while disconnected as the second.

1. Send/Receive > Work Offline, so Outlook stops retrying. Then open the Outbox, delete the message or drag it to Drafts and remove the attachment.
2. If the Work Offline button is blue, click it until it turns white, then Send All.
3. If mail only leaves when you press F9, turn on "Send immediately when connected" under File > Options > Advanced.

The guide also covers the safe-mode test for add-ins: https://www.epifive.com/blog/outlook-stuck-in-outbox-fix-not-sending?utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=group&utm_campaign=sharekit

### 1c. iPhone storage full
**Best for groups like:** iPhone users and tips, parents and families, seniors learning technology.

"iPhone Storage Full" but you cannot find what is using it? Do not delete things at random.

1. Open Settings > General > iPhone Storage and see what is big first.
2. System Data is managed by iOS, so you cannot delete it directly. Offload big apps you rarely use: this keeps their documents and data.
3. Deleted photos stay in Recently Deleted for 30 days, so space only returns after you empty that folder.

Careful: with iCloud Photos on, deleting a photo on one device removes it from all. Details: https://www.epifive.com/blog/iphone-storage-full-system-data-what-to-delete?utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=group&utm_campaign=sharekit

### 3a. (US) "Should I freeze my credit after a data breach, or is a fraud alert enough?"
Question URL: not confirmed, search Quora first.

A freeze and a fraud alert do different jobs. A credit freeze blocks lenders from accessing your credit file until you lift it. It is free at Equifax, Experian and TransUnion, and the CFPB says it does not affect your credit scores. The catch: you must place it at each of the three bureaus separately.

A fraud alert doesn't block access; it only requires lenders to verify your identity before opening credit. An initial alert lasts one year, an extended one seven years, and you only ask one bureau, which notifies the other two.

If your Social Security number was exposed, most people are better off with the freeze, and you can add a fraud alert on top, since the two work together. When you apply for a loan or card, lift the freeze temporarily, ideally at the one bureau the lender will check, then it re-locks. Example: before a car loan in the US, ask the dealer which bureau they pull, lift just that one for a few days.

I wrote the full comparison with steps for each bureau here: https://www.epifive.com/blog/credit-freeze-vs-fraud-alert-us-bureaus?utm_source=quora&utm_medium=answer&utm_campaign=sharekit

### 3b. "Why is my iPhone storage full when I have barely any photos or apps? What is System Data?"
Question URL: not confirmed, search Quora first.

Open Settings > General > iPhone Storage first and let it load. The bar shows what takes space.

"System Data" (older iOS called it "Other") is managed by iOS: caches, logs and temporary files. You cannot delete it directly, and iOS clears cached and temporary files itself when it needs room.

What you can do:
- Offload apps you rarely use. This removes the app but keeps its documents and data, unlike deleting it.
- Check Recently Deleted in Photos. Deleted photos stay there 30 days, so space isn't freed until you empty it.
- If you use iCloud Photos with Optimize iPhone Storage, full-resolution photos live in iCloud. But deleting a photo on one device deletes it everywhere, so back up first.

Do not trust apps that promise to "clean" System Data; Apple gives no such tool.

Step by step: https://www.epifive.com/blog/iphone-storage-full-system-data-what-to-delete?utm_source=quora&utm_medium=answer&utm_campaign=sharekit
